01

The headline: I mapped the wrong shape

My maps assumed an in-house employer buying a screening tool. Staging is a product for recruitment agencies, and the employer is the agency's client, not Tailr's customer.

That single structural difference invalidates most of the commercial lifecycle in the original document and four of the five personas. The operating loop survives much better — that part was mapped from the tailor pipeline, which the agency product genuinely reuses.

02

Process map A vs the seven steps

The operating loop is the part that holds up. lib/agency/steps.ts defines seven workflow steps, and lib/agency/phases.ts wraps them in three derived phases. Aligned by stage of work against my eight-step map, the matches and the holes are both obvious.

04

Two scoring engines now exist

I recommended replacing the prototype's scoring with the tailor pipeline's. The build went the other way and shipped the five-category engine — with integrity guards mine did not have. The result is that Tailr now scores the same kind of judgement two different ways.

 Consumer — lib/scoring.tsAgency — lib/agency/scoring.ts
Strength values1 · 0.6 · 0.25 · 01.0 · 0.7 · 0.4 · 0.0
Requirement weightsmust ×2, nice ×1must ×3, important ×2, nice ×1
CategoriesOne — requirement coverageFive — coverage 45, evidence 25, seniority 10, context 10, confidence 10
Human overrideNoneRecruiter overrides beat parsed evidence, and are part of the stored breakdown
Integrity guardInput hash on the tailor cacheENGINE_VERSION plus an inputs hash — a submission refuses to render if the hash disagrees with the stored breakdown
PresentationA percentageBands only — "fit / strong / very strong". Ordinal ranking was deliberately rejected as implying false precision

The agency engine is the better piece of work, and the band decision is one I would not have made and should have. But docs/EVIDENCE-RULE.md was written precisely because two loops pulling opposite ways is a product problem, and two engines grading evidence on different scales is the same problem in a new place. A candidate can be "transferable" worth 0.6 on one side of the product and 0.7 on the other.

05

The six decisions, answered by the code

I asked you for six decisions. Five were already made — most of them in comments written months ago, and mostly better than my recommendation. Here is each one against what the build does.

1. Is "no auto-reject" a law or a setting?

Settled — a law

I recommended: a law, enforced in code.

The code already saysAnd says it three times over. The prefilter: "Nobody is rejected here… they were never told a scan was happening at all." The match floor: "It narrows what we are willing to claim, which is a statement about us." Right-to-represent: "It never filters, ranks or hides anyone (guardrail test)." Client decisions: "'Not for this role' is a signal, never a removal." There is a guardrail test holding it.

2. Does the employer read a candidate's own Tailr profile?

Settled — as recommended

I recommended: never by default, candidate-initiated only.

The code already saysOpt-in and discoverability are separate flags on match_preferences; every change writes a row to matching_consent_events with a copy version and a surface. Applying writes a consent event first, with a disclosure manifest, before anything else happens. The person's own window onto a role is the recommendation and a snapshot — nothing agency-side.

3. Own surface, or inside the ATS?

Settled — own surface

I recommended: own surface for pilot, one real ATS integration before leaving pilot.

The code already saysOwn surface, with its own sign-in at /agencies/sign-in and a separate client context. No ATS integration exists. For agencies that is a far more defensible choice than it would have been for in-house TA — the agency's system of record is this.

4. Is candidate feedback opt-in, default-on, or mandatory?

Half-built

I recommended: on by default, mandatory in the pilot cohort.

Still openClosure notices exist and are principled — "Ghosting is the recruitment industry's worst habit" — and they tell people the role ended and when their data will be deleted. But they say nothing about why. The requirement-level feedback that would turn a rejection into a career path, which was the strategic hinge of the whole document, is not built. This is the one decision genuinely still yours.

5. What do we charge, and who signs?

Untouched

I recommended: per requisition, annual commitment.

Still openThere is no billing anywhere in the agency code — no plans, no Stripe, no seat model. The only limits are input sizes and retention windows. And my recommendation now needs rethinking anyway: "per requisition" priced to an employer is a different instrument when the buyer is an agency running many clients' roles at once.

6. Fork the prototype, or replace its scoring?

Settled — the other way

I recommended: replace the prototype's scoring with the tailor pipeline's.

The code already saysThe five-category engine shipped and is the only implementation — "the frontend never computes scores". It is a port of the prototype's computeScore() reference. The consumer engine remains untouched, so both now exist. See section 04.

06

What each side has that the other doesn't

Built on staging · absent from my maps

  • Screening calls with consented capture — audio only, the agency is controller, the client never sees the raw transcript, and withdrawal is a cascade that deletes derived evidence and rescores.
  • Interviews and calendar — rounds, offered slots, candidate booking by token, ICS generation.
  • Right to represent — per role, never blanket. Gates one act only: submission to a client.
  • References — a token doorway of their own.
  • Handover pack and close-out — the frozen document that ends Tailr's involvement.
  • Closure notices — everyone the loop was opened with is told the role ended, and when their data goes.
  • Right-to-work checks on two axes, and placements.
  • Notification preferences and an audit view, with suppression re-checked at send time.
  • A derived phase model — no phase column to drift out of sync with reality.

In my maps · not in the build

  • Requirement-level feedback to the candidate — the strategic hinge. Closure notices tell people it ended, not what was missing.
  • The calibration loop — outcomes reweighting the requirement set for a role family. Placements are recorded but nothing feeds back into scoring.
  • Any commercial process at all — no pricing, no pilot structure, no back-test. Map B's four gates have no counterpart in code, which is fine; they are a sales process, not a feature.
  • The in-house employer segment — a real second market the build does not currently serve, and a decision you have implicitly already made.
07

Two things I'd act on

Two scoring engines grading the same kind of judgement

Not urgent, but it compounds. The consumer side tells someone their evidence is "transferable" worth 0.6 of a requirement; the agency side scores the same word at 0.7 inside a five-category model, with recruiter overrides on top. The Evidence Rule exists because contradictions between the two halves of Tailr are a product problem, not a tidiness problem.

What I'd doLeave both running — the agency engine is better and rewriting it would be vandalism. But write down which one is canonical and why, in docs/ next to the Evidence Rule, before a third one appears. If either moves, the answer for candidates and recruiters has to move together.

Main and staging have diverged past the point of a clean port

Staging carries the agency product plus migrations on a timestamp naming scheme; main has numbered migrations that stop at 018 and is missing 019_career_evidence.sql entirely — a file that exists on staging and whose table was applied to production by hand. Main can no longer rebuild its own schema, and the two branches' migration histories no longer reconcile.

What I'd doPort 019 and 020 to main first — that is a ten-minute fix that stops the schema drifting further. Then decide deliberately whether the agency product ever lands on main or stays its own deployable. Right now that decision is being made by default, one unmerged branch at a time.
