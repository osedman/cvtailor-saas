Nobody told you it would be an AI

63% of job seekers have now been interviewed by an AI. Of those, 70% were never told up front that a machine would be evaluating them, and 21% only found out when they joined the call and realised the only face on screen was their own.

That is worth sitting with, because it reframes the whole experience. If your last interview felt strange, stilted, impossible to read, that is not a sign you handled it badly. You were asked to perform without being told what the rules were or who was watching. The awkwardness was built into the format before you sat down.

What actually helps

Ask before you accept the slot

One email: will AI be used at any stage, and does a human review the result? It is a completely reasonable question, and whether they answer it plainly tells you as much about the company as the interview will.

Decide your walk away line in advance

The three most common reasons people left: a recorded video scored with no human involved anywhere (33%), no explanation of how the AI would be used (27%), and being monitored throughout the process (26%). Pick which of those you will not accept while you are calm, not mid call.

Get a name, then use it

Find the actual recruiter and follow up directly once you are done. It puts a person into a loop that otherwise contains none, and it lets someone see how you communicate rather than how you perform to a countdown.

If you stay, treat it as reps

Finding out mid interview that you are being scored by a model is deflating. The most useful thing you can do with the next twenty minutes is run it as practice for the role you actually want, then let it go.

Do not read the silence as a verdict

51% of people who complete an AI interview never hear anything back at all. Not a rejection, not feedback. That is a broken process telling on itself, and it is not information about you.

38% have already walked away from a hiring process because it included AI. Another 12% say they would.
