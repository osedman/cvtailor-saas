## Option A — The AI interview walkout

Strongest and freshest. Forbes ran this a week ago off new Greenhouse data.

- 63% of job seekers have now faced an AI interview, up 13 points in six months
- 38% have walked away from a process because it included AI, another 12% say they would
- 82% were never clearly told an AI would be interviewing them
- 51% who complete an AI interview never hear back at all
- UK specific: 47% of UK job seekers have had an AI interview, 30% walked away
- 74% doubt AI will evaluate them fairly

The human detail is strong: candidates describing it as "awkward and humiliating", talking to a countdown clock and their own reflection, an AI agent cutting them off when they paused to think.

**Angle:** this is the first theme where the advice is not just "cope better", it is "you are allowed to walk, and here is how to tell when you should". Empowering rather than defensive.

## Option B — Everyone's CV now reads the same

Business Insider, March, still building through the year.

- Recruiters saying the resume is "almost worthless because they all read the same" now that anyone can generate one in seconds
- Fake and embellished applications have become hard to tell apart from strong candidates
- Employers shifting to skills verification, working tryouts, and what one analyst calls quiet hiring
- Related: "skills manifesting", candidates inflating resumes because they assume everyone else is

**Angle:** slightly risky for us. It is a real trend and Tailr has a genuine answer (evidence mapped to the job description rather than generated filler), but the theme is adjacent to "AI wrote your CV" and needs careful handling so it does not read as self own.

## Option C — No salary, no application

- 72% are less likely to apply when a posting does not list salary
- 79% say missing pay info makes them question the employer's transparency
- 67% have suspected a posting was fake, misleading, or never meant to be filled

**Angle:** puts the reader back in a position of judgement rather than supplication. A checklist for reading a posting before you spend an hour on it. Weakest tie to Tailr of the three, strongest on goodwill.

---

## Option D — Doomjobbing

Newer term, applying to roles you do not want out of anxiety rather than intent. Sits next to the mental health data: roughly half of job seekers report the search damaging their mental health, with silence and rejection the two worst parts.

**Angle:** the most emotionally resonant and the most differentiated from anything we have sent. Practical takeaway is about application quality over volume, which does tie to Tailr cleanly.

---

## Option E — Both sides stopped trusting each other

- 13% of candidates used generative AI live during an interview, 44% for company research, 41% to generate answers outright
- Employers responding with more verification, more rounds, more unpaid tasks
- Candidates responding by trusting the process less, which pushes more AI use

**Angle:** the arms race framing. Interesting and true, but it is analysis more than advice, and the practical takeaways are thinner than the other four.

---

## Recommendation

Option A. It is the newest, has the hardest numbers, has real quotes with genuine emotional weight, is strongly UK relevant, and gives readers permission to do something rather than just cope. Option D is the best alternative if you want a change of register.
