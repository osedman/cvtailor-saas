Your CV is fighting last year's rules

Last time we wrote about the worst part of applying, not knowing whether a human ever saw your CV. There is a harder version of that problem, and it came up all month. Sometimes something did read it. The rules it read by had already moved.

The consensus forming across r/resumes is that a CV you have not touched in twelve months may now be failing on structure alone, before anyone weighs your actual experience. The one page rule, treated as gospel for a decade, is being retired in favour of length by career stage. Templates that used to parse cleanly no longer do. None of that is a verdict on your work. It is a spec change nobody sent you.

What actually helps

Match length to career stage, not habit

One page under five years of experience, two pages for five to fifteen, two to three at executive level. The one page rule was never meant to apply to everyone, and applying it to a fifteen year career costs you the evidence that makes you credible.

Lead every bullet with action and result

Say what you did and what happened, then stop. "Cut onboarding time by 30% by rebuilding the flow" earns its line. A four part paragraph explaining the background does not.

Save full STAR for the interview

Situation and Task give context a spoken answer needs and a written bullet has no room for. On the page, Action plus Result is the tighter form. In the room, tell the whole story.

Recheck anything you have not opened in a year

Parser behaviour and recruiter search logic shift more than people expect. A CV that quietly worked last year is not automatically a CV that works now, and you will not get told which one you are sending.

Keep the version you upload plain

Dense tables, columns and graphics still break when a portal tries to read them. Save the designed version for when you are handing it to a person directly.

The new length consensus: 1 page under 5 years · 2 pages at 5 to 15 · 2 to 3 at executive
