Entry level now means three to five years

Someone applied for an entry level role that advertised training. In the interview they were told they were a perfect fit. Then they were rejected for not having enough experience. When they asked how much experience an entry level job needs, the answer was three to five years.

It would be comforting to file that under one absurd employer. The numbers say otherwise. 21% of companies have already stopped hiring entry level workers because of AI, and 36% expect to by the end of this year. 31% of employers have raised their experience requirements. Yet only around 11% are actually cutting the roles. The routine work that used to justify a junior hire moved somewhere else, the requirements inflated to cover what was left, and nobody updated the label on the door. If you keep being told you are not experienced enough for a job that is supposed to be someone's first, you are running into a category that stopped meaning what it says.

What actually helps

Treat the years line as a wishlist, not a gate

Someone asked hiring managers directly how those numbers get chosen: what is learned in year twelve that could not be learned in year ten? Nobody had a straight answer. Apply anyway.

Read the tasks, not the tenure

Scroll past the years line to what the job actually asks you to do. If you can evidence the work, the number at the top is far more negotiable than the responsibilities underneath it.

Lead with the judgement, not the admin

The routine tasks that used to make up a junior role are precisely the ones that moved. What is left is coordination, judgement, and dealing with people. If you have done any of that, it belongs at the top.

Favour postings that describe outcomes

A posting written around what the job achieves usually came from someone who thought about the role. A posting built around a years figure often did not. Spend your hours on the first kind.

Do not read a years rejection as a verdict

It is usually a filter someone set quickly and applied before anyone read a word you wrote. That is a fact about their process, not a measurement of what you are capable of.
