The part of your CV you are afraid to write

Last time we wrote about the lines on your CV doing no work, and what happens when you cut them. This is the opposite problem, and it turns out to be the more common one. Not the lines you should remove. The ones you are too worried to put down at all.

It takes a lot of shapes. A job you left after two weeks. A stretch of years spent caring for someone. Work you did that you would rather not name. A period you cannot easily explain in one line. The details differ, but the question underneath is always the same one: what do I do with the bit I am frightened of?

And the first instinct is almost always the same too. Invent something. A business that did not exist, a role reshaped into something safer, a gap quietly papered over. It is an understandable instinct and it is the one genuinely dangerous answer, because an invention is the single thing a background check is built to catch.

What actually helps

Name the period rather than leaving a hole

An unexplained gap does not read as neutral. It invites the reader to guess, and the guess is reliably worse than whatever actually happened. One plain line closes it: a career break, a period of caring, time out for health or study. You are not required to explain further, and most readers do not ask once it has a name.

Look at what the time actually contained

People look at a long gap as one undifferentiated hole, when almost nobody spends years doing nothing. There is usually volunteering, study, a course, a side project, or somebody who would happily act as a reference. It is common to be sitting on real, usable, entirely true material and not see it, because you have already written the whole period off in your head.

Never invent an employer

Reframing what you did is fair. Inventing where you did it is not, and it is the one claim that verification is specifically designed to test. Offers get rescinded at exactly the stage where you have already resigned from the job you had, which is the worst possible moment to be caught out by something you wrote months earlier.

A short stint is a bad fit, not a pattern

Two weeks somewhere, sitting behind seven years of steady work, reads as a job that was not what it said it was. That is ordinary and most people have one. It only becomes a problem when it is left off and then surfaces later, because at that point the story is no longer about the fortnight, it is about the omission.

Take your paperwork to the background check

Automated verification is literal and it is not careful. A changed surname, a hyphenation, a transliterated name or a renamed university is enough to have genuine history returned as unverified. Keep the documents that connect the two versions of your name or your qualification, expect to have to send them twice, and ask for a human the moment a machine says no.

The truth, framed well, holds up. An invention is built to fail at the worst moment.
