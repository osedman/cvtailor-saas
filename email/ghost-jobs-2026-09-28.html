Some of those jobs were never real

One story travelled further than any other this week. Someone spent two years cataloguing fake job listings on LinkedIn, roles posted with no intention of hiring anyone, and counted sixty thousand of them.

It landed because it named something a lot of people already suspected. Next to it sat a recent graduate who had sent more than seventeen hundred applications, tailoring each one and keeping every version to a single page, and had been rewarded with two first round interviews.

Not every silence is a ghost job, and a quiet inbox can still mean the CV needs work. But some of the time you are being ignored by a listing that was never going to hire anybody. The time you spend on those is time taken from the ones that will.

How to spot one before you apply

It has been up for months

A role reposted again and again, or listed as always hiring, is often collecting CVs rather than filling a seat. Favour postings from the last two weeks. Real vacancies tend to close.

Nobody is named

No team, no manager, no sense of what the first three months look like. A job someone genuinely needs filled usually comes with a person who can describe the problem they want solved.

It is not on their own site

Check the company's careers page before applying through a job board. If the role is missing there, treat the listing with suspicion. It takes a minute and can save an hour.

Track where humans reply

Keep a simple note of which applications got any response from a real person. Within a few weeks a pattern shows: certain sizes of company, certain boards, certain routes in. Put more of your effort there.

Fewer, better applications

Ten well matched applications to roles that are clearly live will usually do more than a hundred one click applies. Volume feels productive. It mostly feeds the listings that were never going to call.

Silence is not always a verdict on you. Sometimes nobody was ever there.
