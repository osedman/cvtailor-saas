## Resume format whiplash: what changed

**➜ The one-page rule isn't universal anymore — match length to your career stage.** The emerging consensus this week: one page for 0–5 years, two pages for 5–15, and two-to-three for executive-level. The pushback is aimed squarely at people applying the one-page rule to a 15-year career because a blog told them to in 2019.

**➜ STAR is getting trimmed for resumes.** Commenters are moving to a leaner **Action + Result** for resume bullets — lead with what you did and what happened, and drop the Situation/Task setup. Save full STAR for interviews, where you actually have room to give context.

**➜ Resumes that used to pass are now failing — and it's not you.** The harder claim getting traction: ATS filters and recruiter search logic have shifted enough in the last year that old templates and phrasing don't parse the way they used to. A resume you haven't touched in 12 months may be quietly getting filtered.

**Takeaway: your resume isn't a set-and-forget document. If it's been frozen for a year, it's probably fighting last year's rules.**

## What I'd actually do

- **Match length to seniority, not habit** — one page under 5 years, two for 5–15, two-to-three for senior.
- **Rewrite bullets as Action + Result** — "Cut onboarding time 30% by rebuilding the flow," not a four-part paragraph.
- **Re-check anything older than 12 months** against how ATS parses today — dense tables, columns and graphics still quietly break in portal submissions. Save the pretty designed version for when you're handing it to a human directly.
- **Tailor per role instead of sending one master template** — the "right" length and phrasing depend on the seniority the posting is targeting.

## Also bubbling up this week
