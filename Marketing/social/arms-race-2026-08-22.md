## C. Facebook post

A hiring manager complained recently that every CV he received for one opening was
identical AI written slop, and that he now has to interview people who cannot back
up their own bullet points.

The most upvoted reply did not defend the candidates. It pointed out that his job ad
was AI written, his filter auto rejects anyone who does not mirror its keywords, and
his rejection emails are templates that sometimes still contain the unfilled
variables. People reached for AI because a human had already stopped reading.

Nobody involved wants this. Candidates do not want to sound like everyone else, and
managers do not want to read it. But opting out on your own just means being
filtered out, so everyone keeps feeding the machine.

Here is the part worth paying attention to. One company interviewed its top three
candidates out of hundreds. One could not do the basics he had claimed. One had a CV
so embellished it took real work to establish what he had not done. The third was
honest but so oversold he read as overqualified until he could not answer a simple
question. They hired nobody.

Which means the advantage has quietly moved. It is not a better sounding CV any
more. It is being able to talk about every line on yours for two minutes without
preparation. That is a low bar and very few applications currently clear it.
